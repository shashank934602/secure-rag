# Annual Leave Policy

Aldermoor Freight Ltd. Version 3.0 - effective 1 January 2026. Owner: Head of People.

## 1. Scope

This policy covers paid annual leave (holiday) for all employees. It does not cover sickness absence or other types of leave.

## 2. Leave year

The leave year runs from 1 January to 31 December.

## 3. Entitlement

Full-time employees receive **25 days** of paid annual leave per leave year, plus the **8 UK bank holidays**.

Part-time employees receive a pro-rata entitlement based on their contracted hours. For example, an employee working three days a week receives 15 days plus pro-rata bank holidays.

Employees who join or leave part-way through the leave year receive a pro-rata entitlement for the months worked, rounded up to the nearest half day.

### Long-service leave

After five years of continuous service you receive **one extra day** of annual leave. After ten years you receive **two extra days**, giving a maximum of 27 days plus bank holidays.

## 4. Booking leave

- Request leave in the PeopleHub portal.
- For **three or more consecutive days**, request at least **two weeks in advance**.
- All leave must be approved by your **line manager** before you book travel.
- Shift-based depot staff: no more than two people from the same shift team may be on leave at the same time. Requests are approved in the order they are received.

## 5. Peak-season restriction

Operations staff (depot, warehouse, and driver roles) may not take annual leave between **1 December and 24 December**, our busiest period, unless the Operations Director approves an exception in writing.

## 6. Carrying over leave

You may carry over up to **5 days** of unused leave into the next leave year. Carried-over days must be used by **31 March**; any carried-over days not used by then are lost.

Unused leave above 5 days is lost at the end of the leave year, unless you could not take it because of long-term sickness, in which case contact the People team.

## 7. Leaving the company

If you leave, you are paid for any accrued but untaken leave. If you have taken more leave than you have accrued, the excess is deducted from your final pay.
